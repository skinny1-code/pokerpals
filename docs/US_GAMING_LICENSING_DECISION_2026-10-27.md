## 1. Wyoming — licensing route CLOSED for this business model

The Wyoming Gaming Commission expressly states that **real-money online poker rooms and online casinos are illegal in Wyoming** and that it does not license them. Online sports betting and fantasy sports are a different regulated category. Wyoming's 2025 HB0162 interactive gaming proposal **died in committee on 2025-03-03**. Wyoming 2026 SF0044 took effect on 2026-07-01 and narrowed the private/social gambling exemption: a publicly marketed platform charging poker rake or facilitating monetizable CHIPS wagering does not meet the stated private non-commercial criteria.

- Wyoming Gaming Commission statement: https://gaming.wyo.gov/news-press-releases
- Wyoming licensed online sports betting is not online poker: https://gaming.wyo.gov/OSW
- HB0162 legislative status: https://www.wyoleg.gov/Legislation/2025/HB0162
- 2026 SF0044 enacted restrictions: https://wyoleg.gov/Legislation/2026/SF0044
- Oct 5-6 2026 legislative gaming committee considered restrictions on online casinos/sweepstakes, not an enacted poker licensing route: https://wyoleg.gov/Calendar/20261007/Meeting?id=15163&type=committee

**Do not submit a Wyoming online poker license application: none exists.** A Wyoming LLC does not substitute for gaming operator/vendor approval anywhere and cannot authorize Florida or Wyoming player wagering.

## 3. Actual US poker licensing channels to investigate

### Michigan — licensed gaming platform/supplier partnership

Michigan regulates internet poker through approved commercial casino/tribal operators and licensed platform providers/suppliers. PokerVsPlayer can seek a preliminary **supplier classification/inquiry**, but that does not itself confer independent online-poker operator rights. A regulated casino or tribal operator relationship, licensing, due diligence, testing, and state approval would be needed before offering player wagering.

- Supplier requirements and inquiry forms: https://www.michigan.gov/mgcb/internet-gaming-and-fantasy-contests/supplier
- Regulator portal and supplier contact: https://www.michigan.gov/mgcb/internet-gaming-and-fantasy-contests
- Operator/platform poker examples: https://www.michigan.gov/mgcb/internet-gaming-and-fantasy-contests/authorized-online-gaming-and-sports-betting-platform-providers-in-michigan
- Regulator supplier questions: `mgcb-suppliers@michigan.gov`

**First practical filing:** Supplier Inquiry Form, describing software, non-custodial smart-contract escrow, `CHIPS` trading/transferability, operator-signed settlements, and proposed geographic restrictions. Do not claim an operating partnership or approval.

### Nevada — interactive gaming operator or service provider

Nevada licenses interactive gaming including poker. The published **operator of interactive gaming** license fee is **$500,000 initially for two years**, then **$250,000 annually**; a **service provider** license fee is **$1,000 annually**, but that service license does **not** authorize the service provider to independently run a poker room. Investigation, partner requirements, suitability, and platform/manufacturer approvals are separate.

- Nevada fee schedule: https://www.gaming.nv.gov/divisions/tax-license-division/license-fees-and-tax-rate-schedule/
- License forms: https://www.gaming.nv.gov/about-us/forms-and-applications/

## 4. Platform features that REQUIRE legal classification before real-money use

1. The existing ERC-20 CHIPS token is intended to have tradable value and funds game pots; **crypto is not a statutory exception** to poker gambling regulation. Do not assume renaming CHIPS as 'points', using Robinhood Chain, or registering a Wyoming entity makes monetary wagering legal.
2. Player escrow custody/control, casino/operator-signed winning payouts, rake, liquidity, token distribution/vesting, and APR staking raise separate gaming, financial services, AML/sanctions, and promotional questions. Licensed operators may require fiat/stablecoin substitutions or prohibit tokenized buy-ins entirely.
3. Resolve whether the game runs purely as a technology supplier product under a licensed operator, rather than a standalone PokerVsPlayer branded operator.
4. Technical evidence needed: independently reviewed fairness/RNG/shuffle, anti-cheat/collusion, logs/auditability, identity and age verification, true device geolocation, responsible gaming, dispute procedures, settlement/recovery controls, and wallet/key security. Wallet address alone is not geolocation or identity.
5. Block **all real-money activity** until licensing/authorization explicitly supports the contemplated jurisdiction, brand, token, and economic flow. Existing Arbitrum testnet/Robinhood testnet experiments remain nonredeemable testing only.
6. A genuine play-for-fun demo should use **nontransferable, nonredeemable, noncash-value** credits with no monetary prizes, no public CHIPS market connection, and no paid rake. Review the intended demo geography and mechanics before publishing it.
